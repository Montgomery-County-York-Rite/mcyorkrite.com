Mark Master Mason is the first degree conferred in a Royal Arch Chapter.
Its symbolism is drawn from the working life of the operative craftsman:
the marking of finished stones so that each could be identified, credited
to its maker, and fitted to its intended place in the building.

## History

The custom at the heart of the degree is genuinely ancient. Medieval
operative masons chiseled personal marks into the stones they dressed,
partly as a signature of workmanship, partly so that payment could be
matched to work, and partly to distinguish sound stone from flawed stone
at the building site. Marked stones survive in cathedrals and abbeys
across Europe, and marked stones have been recovered from early American
building sites as well.

Ceremonial degrees built around this practice appeared in the eighteenth
century, and they arose in more than one place independently. An Irish
"Mark Man" and "Mark Master" tradition developed alongside the Craft
degrees, a Scottish tradition attached the Mark to Royal Arch
qualification, and in the American colonies and the new United States the
Mark was being worked well before the close of the eighteenth century.
These parallel developments share the same core imagery even where their
legends differ, which is why no single founding place or date is claimed
for the degree.

In the American York Rite the Mark Master degree settled into place as
the natural beginning of the Capitular system: the candidate continues
the story of a Fellowcraft workman, and the degree's keystone symbolism
links the Craft Lodge narrative to the Temple story that the Chapter
degrees carry forward.

## In the York Rite

The Mark degree is conferred as the first of the four degrees of the
[Chapter of Royal Arch Masons](/about/chapter/). It prepares the way for
[Past Master](/about/degrees/past-master/),
[Most Excellent Master](/about/degrees/most-excellent-master/), and
[Royal Arch Mason](/about/degrees/royal-arch-mason/). A fuller overview of
all four degrees is on the [degree histories](/about/degrees/) page.

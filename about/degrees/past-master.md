Past Master (sometimes called the Virtual Past Master degree) is the
second degree conferred in a Royal Arch Chapter. It exists because of an
old constitutional idea rather than a legend of its own: in the early
years of Royal Arch Masonry, the exaltation was regarded as so closely
connected to the governance of the Craft that only a Mason who had
actually presided over a lodge, an installed Master, was considered
eligible to receive it.

## History

Early American Royal Arch regulations carried that requirement forward.
The difficulty was practical: it would have excluded every worthy
brother who had not yet served as Master of his lodge. Chapters resolved
this by conferring a short ceremonial degree, the Virtual Past Master,
which qualified the candidate in the constitutional sense without making
him a past presiding officer of a lodge. The practice was well
established in American chapters by the close of the eighteenth century
and remains standard in the York Rite today.

The requirement itself was handled differently elsewhere. In England,
Royal Arch membership came to be tied to the Craft degrees alone, and
after the Grand Chapter's 2004 decisions (described on the
[Chapter page](/about/chapter/)), no installed or presiding requirement
applies there. The United States system, working under its own Grand
Chapters and the General Grand Chapter, retained the Past Master degree
as a distinct step.

The degree also carries genuine instruction of its own: its lessons
concern the duties and responsibilities of the chair (presiding,
teaching, and serving), which is why it has kept its place rather than
becoming a mere formality.

## In the York Rite

Past Master is conferred between
[Mark Master Mason](/about/degrees/mark-master-mason/) and
[Most Excellent Master](/about/degrees/most-excellent-master/) in the
[Chapter](/about/chapter/). The full sequence of Capitular degrees is
listed on the [degree histories](/about/degrees/) page.

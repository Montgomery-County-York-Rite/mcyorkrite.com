Most Excellent Master is the third degree conferred in a Royal Arch
Chapter. Its setting is a moment of triumph: the completion and
dedication of King Solomon's Temple, when the work begun in the Craft
degrees is at last finished and the builders gather to celebrate what
their hands have made.

## History

Unlike the Mark and Royal Arch degrees, whose roots reach back to
eighteenth-century Britain and Ireland, Most Excellent Master is an
American development. It emerged in the United States in the late
eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, as American Masons organized
their Capitular system and the degrees that would carry the Temple story
from its building to its loss and recovery. The degree found its place
in the monitorial literature of the period — the era of Thomas Smith
Webb's widely used Masonic monitors — and from there spread through the
Grand Chapters of the several states.

Its historical content is drawn from the biblical account of the
Temple's completion under Solomon: the finish of the work, the
sanctifying of the building, and the bringing in of the Ark. The degree
thus answers a narrative need within the York Rite — it marks the
fulfilment of the building story before the destruction and loss that
the later degrees lament and repair.

## In the York Rite

Most Excellent Master follows [Past Master](/about/degrees/past-master/)
and prepares the candidate for the crowning
[Royal Arch degree](/about/degrees/royal-arch-mason/) in the
[Chapter](/about/chapter/). The Capitular sequence is summarized on the
[degree histories](/about/degrees/) page.

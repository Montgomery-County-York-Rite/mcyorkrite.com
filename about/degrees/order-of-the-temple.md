The Order of the Temple is the final and crowning order of the York
Rite. Its theme is the making of a knight after the manner of the
medieval Poor Fellow-Soldiers of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon,
the Knights Templar, and its ceremony brings the pilgrim of the whole
Rite, from the building of the first Temple onward, to a solemn
conclusion at the foot of the cross.

## History of the medieval order

The historical Knights Templar were founded around 1119, when a small
band of knights bound themselves to protect Christian pilgrims
traveling in the Holy Land after the First Crusade. Under the patronage
of Bernard of Clairvaux the order received papal recognition, grew into
an international institution with fortresses, estates, and a banking
network across Europe, and served as the standing military force of the
crusader states. After the fall of the last crusader strongholds, the
order's wealth and independence made it a target: King Philip IV of
France moved against the Templars in 1307, and Pope Clement V suppressed
the order at the Council of Vienne in 1312, transferring much of its
property to the Hospitallers, the Knights of St. John, whose
[Masonic commemoration](/about/degrees/order-of-malta/) precedes this
order in the Commandery.

## History of the Masonic order

The Masonic Templar order is a commemoration, not a continuation. It
emerged in the mid-eighteenth century, first in Europe and soon after in
colonial America, as chivalric ceremonies attached themselves to
Masonry; by the early nineteenth century the American Commanderies had
organized their series of orders under the Grand Encampment of Knights
Templar of the United States, established in 1826.

The relationship between the medieval order and the Masonic one is a
matter the Grand Encampment addresses directly, and the
[Commandery page](/about/commandery/) preserves its statement: claims
that Freemasonry was founded by the Templars, or that fugitive
Templars took refuge within it, are acknowledged as theories, with no
proof to justify them. The Order of the Temple asks its knights to
carry on the *spirit* of the historical order: courage, discipline, and
service in defense of the Christian faith.

## In the York Rite

The Order of the Temple completes the series conferred by the
[Commandery](/about/commandery/), after the
[Illustrious Order of the Red Cross](/about/degrees/illustrious-order-of-the-red-cross/),
the [Passing Order of St. Paul](/about/degrees/order-of-st-paul/), and
the [Order of Malta](/about/degrees/order-of-malta/). All York Rite
degree and order histories are indexed on the
[degree histories](/about/degrees/) page.
